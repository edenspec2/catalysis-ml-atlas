MILO LAB

Ten minutes, then a habit.

This is a curated map of homogeneous catalysis × ML — not the whole field, and not a PDF library. Zotero keeps the files. The atlas keeps what the paper is for: n, validation, and stack layer.

Three shelves

  1. Shared atlas — the curated core (summaries, stack). Only a curator promotes a paper here.
  2. My shelf — To Read, Read, and a private note on this browser. Export → Back up my shelf moves it to another laptop. It is not synced across the group.
  3. Lab queue — Propose a DOI as a GitHub issue. Someone reviews it, then it can enter the atlas.

First ten minutes

  1. In the Library, pick a topic in the sidebar. The table button beside Export shows data size, validation and stack layer for deciding what to read.
  2. Open the 3D Map. Drag to rotate, Shift-drag to pan. The Topic menu holds the scientific cuts (Milo neighborhood, descriptor tools, computational stack).
  3. Pick one paper you need this week. Tap the bookmark to keep it in To Read. When you finish it, tap the circle and write a one-line note.

Add a paper the lab can see

  1. Find Papers → paste a DOI → Search.
  2. Add it if you want it on this device today.
  3. Open the paper → More about this paper → Propose for the shared atlas. Fill the one-sentence why, chemistry, stack, n, and validation. Do not paste the abstract.
  4. Put the PDF in the group Zotero collection. Do not attach publisher PDFs to GitHub.

Export into the tools you already use

Open a list, then use Export for BibTeX or your reading notes as Markdown. Similarity links in the 3D map are not citations.

What this is not

  • Not an exhaustive literature search.
  • Not a replacement for Zotero, Eln, or group Slack.
  • Not a dump of every catalysis-ML paper. If a DOI does not belong in this core, it can stay on your shelf.
